# Balance report

Generated by `npm run balance` from the numbers in `src/` (2026-09-25). The formulas are in `tools/balance/model.js`, and `tests/balance-model.test.js` checks them against the running game: prices, the morning bill, harvest weights and what customers order.

A game day is 20 minutes of real time. Where the code cannot say what a player does, the report assumes: one customer served every 30 s by the player alone (40 a day) and 20 more a day for each crew member, hand-trimming at 90% of a perfect trim, and footfall at 1.0 (weather, weekends and the holiday week roughly cancel out). Money is after tax: 15% excise on every sale, 10% business tax on a month's takings and 20% on what a month takes over $60,000, paid monthly.

## Findings

1. **Quality tops out early.** A plant under the LED panel, fed once and fully cured, already reaches quality 100 (it starts at 79 and curing adds up to 22 points in about 42 s). So the quality bonus of every lamp after it (HPS rig (+18), Quantum board (+26), Full-spectrum array (+34)), both curing racks (+10 and +20 to the cap) and the Genetics lab (+10, $12,000) add nothing to a price. The lamps still earn their keep through yield and speed; the racks only save a few seconds and the genetics lab nothing at all. Connoisseurs, who want 70+, are always satisfied.

2. **Diesel Drift is outclassed.** It unlocks at level 6 but grows less value per slot per day ($83.1 under an LED) than Green Widow (level 4, $104.6). A player who works that out never plants it.

3. **Reputation gates pace the first weeks.** Every sale adds 1 to 4 rep plus a point for every 25 of quality (about 5.5 at quality 80), and nothing takes it away except penalties, so a shop serving its first 20 customers a day gains about 110 rep a day, and more as it grows. The simulated player passes the gates on these days: Lounge licence (200) day 9, Firearms licence (240 and level 4) day 10, Connoisseur permit (320) day 11, Late-hours licence (400) day 11, Brand registration (480) day 12, Export licence (960 and level 10) day 25. Rep stops mattering to price at 100 (+10%) and to footfall at 133.

4. **The markup slider is free money while stock is short.** With too little to sell (a 6-slot tent under an LED), the best markup is 130%: $238 a day against $132 at 100%, because every gram sells anyway and the lost customers were never going to be served. The slider only starts to cost money once there is more stock than customers.

5. **The basement line is a solid mid-game side business.** It turns out about 360 packs of 20 a game day (the cigarette maker sets the pace), and the Corner Tobacconist takes every one at 50% of shop price with no cap: $2,340 a day, $792 after tax, seed, materials, the basement rent and the two staff it needs. The licence costs $3,000 and needs level 8. The simulated player could first get it on day 20 (6.7 hours of play), when the shop itself was netting $1,986 a day, so it adds 40% to the income at that point. A fully built shop nets $4,480 a day. Over 90 days the player with the licence ends with $313,986 against $276,449. The model sells every pack to the tobacconist; the counter (full price, to customers who ask) and the tablet rounds (about 125% of it, for the drive) pay more for the packs they take.

6. **Levels run out in the mid-game.** The last thing a level unlocks is at level 10, and the simulated player gets there on day 25 (8.3 hours of play). After that a level-up unlocks nothing, and the player was level 20 by day 90. XP is an eighth of every sale plus a point per gram harvested, so it grows with the shop, and each level asks for more than the last: 60 XP for level 2, 2740 for level 10.

7. **The customers run out before the grow room does.** With the billboard and full reputation about 83 customers come a game day, wanting about 400 g. A fully upgraded slot grows 26.1 g a day, so about 16 slots already cover every customer the shop can get (the 16-slot tent). The bigger tents past that, and Cultivation permit III ($6,000), only pay through side channels: the till's walk-up sales (12 a day), deliveries, the van, street deals, export contracts and the branch. (The simulated player, which only buys what pays back on its own, stopped at 12 slots: the permit earns nothing until the tent it unlocks is bought as well, and the same goes for the Auto-waterer that the Hydroponic loop needs.)

8. **Money piles up late.** The simulated player had bought everything that pays back within 40 days by day 26 (8.7 hours of play) and then banked about $4,256 a day, ending day 90 with $276,449. The tax on big months (20% instead of 10% on what a month takes over $60,000) costs that shop about $622 a day; a young shop never reaches it. The only big things left to buy are the Green Leaf buy-out ($180,000, which then pays daily), the export licence and the cosmetic upgrades.

9. **Robberies are a tax on a full till.** Random events come about every two minutes and 7% of them are a robbery once the till and tip jar hold $30 (3% with the guard on patrol): about 0.7 robberies a game day. A robber takes whatever is in the till and the tip jar, and a gunman goes for the vault next. A player who empties the till into the vault once a day loses on average about 33% of a day's cash takings to robberies; one who empties it after every few sales loses almost nothing. The vault readout turns amber, and a warning comes once a day, when the till and the tip jar hold $250 or more.
